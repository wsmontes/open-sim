# Vancouver traffic counts

Checked 2026-10-03. The [City collection program](https://vancouver.ca/streets-transportation/traffic-count-data.aspx) describes hourly permanent counts and manual turning movements. The open-data catalog provides 659 approximate locations and links, not volumes.

The official [VanMap service](https://maps.vancouver.ca/server/rest/services/GDH/Short_Term_Intersection_Movement_Counts/MapServer) exposes approach tables for 2019, 2018 and 2011–2017. Layer 27 record 1 reports a 2019-09-05 AM peak starting 07:30, total 717 vehicles and 16 pedestrians. The server identifies Pacific time with daylight saving. Neither study endtime nor peak-hour factor is an observation duration. The AM peak is an hourly aggregate, not speed. However, JSON and GeoJSON queries with explicit returnGeometry=true both return absent/null geometry, including layer 28. The newer 2024/2023 layers expose report records, not these numeric approach fields.

The saved official sample is raw. It is rejected for road calibration because its location has not been verified. Street names and approach labels alone do not establish a directed road position; no automatic crosswalk, timestamp conversion or fictitious count capture is shipped. `traffic-count-match-report.json` records zero matches and the actual rejection. Dynamic congestion remains unavailable.

Local normalized observations with coordinates, explicit UTC interval, count class and reported source provenance can be imported through tools/vancouver-counts.ts into the existing recorded-observation format. The pure calibration matches ground-level compatible road segments within 25 m and published direction within 30 degrees, only inside the actual observed interval. Equal-distance ambiguous edges are rejected. Rates are observations/hour and require an explicit visual sampling scale before agent generation. Aggregate vehicle counts do not create a truck share or speed. Repeated overlapping records use maximum declared volume rather than adding unknown duplicate lanes. Historic measurements are never silently applied to today's traffic.
